When It's Time to Modernize

The application still works, but some of the technology is outdated.

Legacy doesn't always mean obsolete. Often, it's business-critical software that still serves your team, but aging technology makes everyday work and future improvements harder.

RISK & RELIABILITY

Every change feels risky.

Outdated dependencies, tightly connected code, or limited test coverage make even small improvements difficult to ship with confidence.

INTEGRATION CHALLENGES

New tools don't fit easily.

Your application needs to exchange information with other services, but its current architecture makes integrations difficult.

CHANGING USER NEEDS

Users need more than the old workflows.

Interfaces and processes that once made sense now create extra work for staff, customers, and administrators.

TECHNICAL DEBT

Maintenance is crowding out progress.

Too much engineering time goes toward keeping things running instead of delivering useful improvements.

Find the Right Path

Choosing the Right Legacy Modernization Approach

The right choice depends on what's making your application difficult to maintain or improve. Here are some signs that can help guide the conversation.

Refactoring

Consider refactoring when the application still meets your core needs, but complex code, aging dependencies, or limited test coverage make even small changes risky. You can improve maintainability without changing what the application is meant to do.

Replatforming

Consider replatforming when the application itself is still valuable, but the platform it runs on is holding back upgrades, support, or future development. The goal is to improve the technical foundation while keeping essential functionality.

Re-Architecting

Consider re-architecting when recurring performance, integration, or development problems stem from how the application is structured. A more substantial redesign may be justified when smaller fixes won't address the underlying constraints.

Migrating to Modern Infrastructure

Consider infrastructure migration when hosting, deployment, reliability, or operational requirements have outgrown the current setup. This can change how the application runs without requiring a complete product rewrite.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

From deciding whether to modernize to estimating scope, here are some of the questions teams ask about working with an established application.

01

What is legacy application modernization?

Legacy application modernization is the process of improving an existing software system so it can better meet current technical and business needs. That might involve refactoring code, updating frameworks, changing infrastructure, improving the user experience, or introducing integrations. It doesn't always require replacing the entire application.

02

Why modernize legacy applications?

An application can still serve users while becoming increasingly difficult or expensive to maintain. Modernization can address outdated dependencies, technical debt, integration limitations, and usability problems before they prevent useful changes or create larger operational issues.

03

When is the right time to modernize?

It's worth evaluating when routine changes are taking too long, important dependencies are outdated, the application struggles to support new requirements, or users are working around limitations. An assessment can help distinguish urgent risks from improvements that can happen gradually.

04

What are the common modernization approaches?

Common approaches include refactoring existing code, replatforming parts of an application, changing its architecture, updating infrastructure, improving interfaces, and connecting it to modern services. The appropriate choice depends on the application's condition, business priorities, available resources, and tolerance for disruption.

05

Do we need to rewrite our application from scratch?

Not necessarily. Many applications can be improved through targeted upgrades and incremental changes. A complete rebuild may be appropriate in some situations, but it's a significant decision that should follow a careful review of the existing system and the outcomes you need.

06

How long does legacy application modernization take?

It depends on the condition and complexity of the codebase, how much is changing, and what needs to stay operational throughout the work. A focused improvement can be smaller in scope than a multi-phase architecture or infrastructure project. We work with you to establish priorities and plan an appropriate sequence.

07

How much does legacy application modernization cost?

Costs depend on the application's size, technical condition, test coverage, integrations, and the modernization goals. Rather than assuming every application needs a complete rebuild, we start by understanding what matters and help prioritize work around your constraints and budget.
